Everything, on one screen

Per-process firewall

One click and it's offline

Not a rule to write. Not a wizard. A Block button next to the program's name, and it stops talking to the internet immediately — until you say otherwise.

Right-click any process for details, connections, limits, or to pin it to the top of the list.

Speed limits

Or leave it online, on a leash

Cap download and upload separately, in KB/s or MB/s. Permanently, or for the next 30 minutes while you're in a call.

The limit lifts itself when the time is up. You don't have to remember.

Connection inspector

Where is it actually sending data?

Open any program and see the split — HTTPS, QUIC, DNS — plus the remote hosts it's talking to, resolved to real names instead of bare IPs.

The moment you look, you learn something about your own PC.

System-wide blocking

Ads and trackers, for the whole PC

Blocking happens at the DNS layer, so it covers every browser and every app that isn't a browser. Extensions can't do that.

Ships with a large built-in list. Add your own domains, or import a list you already trust.

Security alerts

Told the first time, not the hundredth

When a program reaches the internet for the first time, starts accepting incoming connections, or your DNS servers change — you get a notification, even when netvis is in the tray.

Most days it's quiet. The day it isn't, you'll want to know.

Built to stay out of the way

Two tabs. That's the whole program.

Processes is the live view. Settings is every switch netvis has, on one page — protection, notifications, startup, appearance. Nothing is buried three menus deep.

Light, dark, or follow Windows. It remembers what you picked.

Your traffic never leaves your computer

netvis analyses everything locally. The sites you visit, the programs you run, the data you move — none of it is uploaded, because there is nowhere for it to go. No account. No telemetry. No analytics.

The only thing that ever leaves is an anonymous hardware ID, once at startup, to check your license.
